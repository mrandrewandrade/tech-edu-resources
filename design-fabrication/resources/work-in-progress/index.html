Work in Progress | Free Online Design & Fabrication Resources

Working notes for ideas, tutorials, software, models, and open collections

Work-in-progress research notes for free and mixed-access design and fabrication resources. Every entry requires further teacher, technical, licensing, and classroom review.

Work in progress · Research notes · Not fully reviewed

WarningWorking draft: verify before classroom use

This directory was assembled from a broad initial online research pass. It has not received complete editorial, technical, licensing, accessibility, age-suitability, or classroom review. Entries are leads, not endorsements. Descriptions may be incomplete, access conditions may change, and important limitations may be missing. A teacher must open and assess each resource before assigning it, and students must verify every claim, file, dimension, licence, and machine setting they use.

Use these working notes to find ideas, learn a process, inspect how something works, or locate material that may support an original design after verification. The strongest links also appear inside the lessons where students need them.

Last reviewed: October 5, 2026. Websites, prices, account rules, and licences change. Confirm the current terms before classroom use.

ImportantFree access is not the same as permission to copy

A page that is free to view may contain paid files, copyrighted photographs, restricted trademarks, or models with different licences. For every reused asset, record the creator, page title, URL, access date, and licence. Check whether attribution, adaptation, redistribution, or commercial use is allowed. Measure and test downloaded geometry before fabrication. A community upload is not proof that dimensions, safety, ownership, or printability are correct.

How the access labels work

Label Meaning
Open source / open content Source or content is available under a stated open licence. The licence still has conditions.
Free access Useful material can be viewed or used without payment. Reuse rights vary.
Free account Core access is free, but registration may be required. Protect student privacy and use board-approved accounts.
Mixed Free and paid material appear together, or some features require payment. Filter carefully.

Teacher review is required for public marketplaces, community galleries, comments, advertising, external downloads, and age-appropriate content. Machine-specific safety instruction and operating procedures follow school board guidelines and the course information provided by the teacher; students in Mr. Andrade’s classes use Google Classroom for that material.

Use these sources to identify needs, compare existing solutions, study mechanisms, and read build evidence. They are starting points for investigation, not answer banks.

iFixit Teardowns, repair sequences, fasteners, and serviceability Free access. Device-specific hazards still apply.
Google Patents Product history, mechanisms, drawings, and claims Free access. A patent drawing is not permission to copy a protected invention.
Etsy Commercial precedents, product language, and user-review themes Mixed marketplace. Analyze, do not copy sellers’ work.
Glowforge Catalog Laser-cut product categories, layers, and construction ideas Mixed. Visibility does not grant file or reproduction rights.

Use the N.I.C.E. Design Process to turn investigation into criteria, alternatives, tests, and revisions. The Good Idea, Better Product challenge provides a complete reverse-engineering assignment.

WarningOnline examples are not operating procedures

Online feeds, speeds, toolpaths, printer profiles, laser settings, electrical circuits, and material claims are examples, not universal instructions. Machine-specific safety instruction, operating procedures, assessments, and authorization are kept outside this public website. Follow your school board’s safety guidelines and the course information provided by your teacher. Students in Mr. Andrade’s classes can find that information in Google Classroom. Treat online values as hypotheses: simulate where appropriate, measure, make a controlled test, and investigate unexpected evidence.

A simple student source record

For every external reference, save:

  1. creator or organization;
  2. page, product, project, or file title;
  3. direct URL and access date;
  4. access label and stated licence;
  5. what you learned from it;
  6. what you reused, adapted, or deliberately changed;
  7. what you verified through measurement, testing, or another source.

Do not submit a folder of links as research. Turn sources into evidence, criteria, alternatives, decisions, tests, and an original result.
