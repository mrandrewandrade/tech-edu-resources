Laser Cutter Calibration | Material Thickness, Kerf & Fit Tests

A measurable classroom workflow for machine checks, stock measurement, kerf, slots, holes and fit decisions.

Calibration is not one magic kerf value. It is a short, recorded test that connects this sheet, this machine condition, this geometry, and this intended fit.

1 · Check machine2 · Measure stock3 · Verify scale4 · Cut a test5 · Measure result6 · Choose + record fit

Machine check or material calibration?

Machine check

Confirm the machine can locate and focus on the material, the bed and optics are ready, and the artwork aligns as expected. This is about machine condition and setup.

  • Material is flat and supported.
  • Focus point lands on the material, away from an edge or hole.
  • Camera alignment is reasonable.
  • Approved settings and ventilation procedure are available.

Material calibration

Measure how one stock-and-setting combination changes size and fit. This is about the result of the process.

  • Actual thickness and batch are recorded.
  • A known drawing dimension imports at 100%.
  • Cut width and finished dimensions are measured.
  • Slots and holes are tested against the real mating object.

Glowforge’s current guidance explains that Set Focus measures the head-to-material distance at a selected point and can improve focus and camera placement; it also advises selecting a point on the material, not close to an edge. If Set Focus is not used, autofocus runs before a print and can fall back to the selected material thickness if measurement fails. Use those features as machine setup—not as a substitute for a fit coupon. See Glowforge: Alignment and Glowforge: About Autofocus.

1 · Measure the actual sheet

Plywood sold as 1/4 inch may not measure 6.35 mm. Measure at several locations with callipers, including near—but not on—a visibly damaged edge. Record the minimum, maximum and the value used to design the test.

Material thicknessRecord a measured range, not a catalogue assumption. Ruler / scale checkConfirm millimetres and 100% import before cutting. Dimension verificationCompare a known drawing dimension to the finished result.

2 · Understand what kerf changes

The beam removes a narrow band centred on the path. With the path drawn on the intended boundary, an outside part tends to finish smaller and an inside opening tends to finish larger. The magnitude varies, so do not copy a universal number.

BRM’s current kerf and kerf-compensation explanation is a useful process reference. BRM also hosts a free kerf gauge SVG, credited there to Nameek and Elplatt, and explicitly tells users to check scale after import. Use it as another test method, not as a pre-approved local setting.

Ponoko’s 2011 article, Figuring out kerf for precision parts, is included only as a historical/conceptual example of test-and-revise thinking. Do not copy its old settings or service-specific assumptions.

Kerf testMeasure a repeated cut rather than guessing from one edge. Tab fitTest the male feature and its orientation. Slot fitStart from measured thickness, then compare a small series. Four-fit seriesName the function: loose, sliding, snug or press. Hole fitTest the actual bottle, dowel or fastener—not only a calliper.

3 · Cut, measure and decide

  1. Open one test SVG in Inkscape and confirm document units, viewBox scale and a known dimension.
  2. Label the coupon with material, batch, date, machine and revision before cutting.
  3. Use the instructor-approved machine setup for this material.
  4. Let the coupon cool and measure it consistently; do not force a calliper into a soft or angled edge.
  5. Test the real mating object in every candidate feature.
  6. Select the fit that serves the function. A press fit is not automatically “more accurate” than a sliding fit.
  7. Update the source dimension, save the record and repeat only the smallest necessary test.
Warning

Kerf compensation changes geometry; it does not correct loose belts, damaged optics, poor focus, warped stock, unapproved settings or an incorrectly scaled import. Stop and resolve a machine-check problem before interpreting the coupon.
