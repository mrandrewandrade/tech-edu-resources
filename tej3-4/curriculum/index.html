0. Orientation, Safety, and Design

What students learn Why it matters
Work safely, follow a design process, and document decisions and sources. Safe, documented work is the foundation for every build, investigation, and engineering decision.

Subsections: Safety; design process; documentation; privacy and attribution

Coming out of this section, students can: Work safely, document decisions, cite sources, and keep a usable design record.

1. Numbers, Units, and Calculator Skills

What students learn Why it matters
Use number systems, units, engineering notation, estimates, and a scientific calculator. Units, scale, estimation, and calculator fluency make technical results understandable and trustworthy.

Subsections: Number systems; units; significant figures; engineering notation; calculator workflow

Coming out of this section, students can: Convert technical quantities, use engineering notation, estimate results, and present calculations clearly.

2. Electrical Fundamentals

What students learn Why it matters
Explain and calculate electrical quantities, power, energy, sources, and loads. These quantities explain how electrical systems transfer energy and why components behave as they do.

Subsections: Charge; voltage; current; resistance; power and energy; AC and DC

Coming out of this section, students can: Explain and calculate voltage, current, resistance, power, energy, opens, shorts, and source behaviour.

3. DC Circuit Analysis

What students learn Why it matters
Analyze and verify series, parallel, and mixed direct-current circuits. Circuit analysis turns schematics into predictions that can be checked by simulation and measurement.

Subsections: Ohm's law; series and parallel circuits; Kirchhoff's laws; dividers; troubleshooting

Coming out of this section, students can: Solve and verify series, parallel, and mixed circuits and diagnose common faults.

4. Building, Schematics, and Measurement

What students learn Why it matters
Build readable circuits and compare calculated, simulated, and measured results. Readable schematics, careful construction, and correct measurement make troubleshooting possible.

Subsections: Breadboards; schematics; simulation; multimeter use; measurement uncertainty

Coming out of this section, students can: Build from a schematic, measure safely, compare predicted and measured values, and locate wiring errors.

5. Components and Applied Circuits

What students learn Why it matters
Select and apply common components in useful low-voltage circuits. Component knowledge lets students select, connect, protect, and test useful electronic circuits.

Subsections: Sensors; batteries; capacitors; diodes; transistors; timers; motors and relays

Coming out of this section, students can: Design and test sensor, timing, switching, power-supply, and actuator-interface circuits.

6. Digital Logic and Inputs

What students learn Why it matters
Create stable digital inputs and analyze combinational and sequential logic. Stable inputs and Boolean logic connect physical switches and sensors to dependable digital decisions.

Subsections: Logic levels; pull-ups and pull-downs; debouncing; logic gates; truth tables; state

Coming out of this section, students can: Build stable switch inputs, complete truth tables, analyze logic, and create a small digital system.

7. Fabrication and PCBs

What students learn Why it matters
Turn a tested prototype into a durable fabricated or manufactured result. Fabrication turns a tested design into a durable, repeatable physical product.

Subsections: Soldering; connectors; PCB design; manufacturing files; enclosures and fabrication

Coming out of this section, students can: Prepare a manufacturable design and produce a soldered, printed, laser-cut, or machined result.

8. Computer Systems

What students learn Why it matters
Assemble, commission, maintain, upgrade, and troubleshoot computer hardware. Computer hardware work connects electrical principles to real systems, maintenance, repair, and reuse.

Subsections: Components; power; assembly; startup; Linux installation; upgrades and troubleshooting

Coming out of this section, students can: Identify, assemble, commission, benchmark, troubleshoot, upgrade, and document a computer.

9. Linux and Operating Systems

What students learn Why it matters
Use and administer a Linux system safely from the desktop and command line. Operating-system knowledge gives students control over files, software, permissions, and system behaviour.

Subsections: Desktop use; files and permissions; command line; software; services; backup and recovery

Coming out of this section, students can: Install and use Linux, manage files and permissions, run commands, and complete basic administration.

10. Programming

What students learn Why it matters
Write, test, debug, and document programs for calculations, data, and control. Programming turns a process or calculation into a repeatable tool that can handle inputs and errors.

Subsections: Algorithms; variables; conditions; loops; functions; files; testing and debugging

Coming out of this section, students can: Write, test, debug, and document programs that calculate, model, process data, or control a task.

11. Microcontrollers

What students learn Why it matters
Connect code to protected sensors, inputs, outputs, communication, and actuators. Physical computing joins code, sensors, outputs, timing, and electrical protection in one system.

Subsections: Inputs and outputs; sensors; timing; communication; actuators; state machines; data logging

Coming out of this section, students can: Create a protected sensor-and-actuator system using a microcontroller or single-board computer.

12. Networking and Administration

What students learn Why it matters
Explain networks and operate small classroom services using defensive practices. Networking explains how devices communicate and how small services are operated safely.

Subsections: Addresses; protocols; diagnostics; secure remote access; services; users and backups

Coming out of this section, students can: Configure and diagnose a small network or classroom service using defensive administration practices.

13. Data Science and Machine Learning

What students learn Why it matters
Prepare, visualize, model, and evaluate non-personal data responsibly. Data science helps students turn measurements into evidence while recognizing uncertainty and bias.

Subsections: Data preparation; visualization; regression; classification; model evaluation; responsible use

Coming out of this section, students can: Clean, visualize, model, and evaluate a non-personal dataset and explain the limits of the result.

14. Language Models and Applied AI

What students learn Why it matters
Explain, use, ground, and evaluate small language-model applications responsibly. Understanding language models helps students use, test, and evaluate artificial intelligence responsibly.

Subsections: Tokens; prediction; small models; prompting; grounding; evaluation; responsible use

Coming out of this section, students can: Build or evaluate a small grounded artificial-intelligence application with documented tests and limits.

15. Culminating Projects

What students learn Why it matters
Plan, build or simulate, test, revise, and explain an integrated technology project. A culminating project combines design, construction, programming, testing, revision, and communication.

Subsections: Requirements; design; build or simulation; testing; revision; technical communication

Coming out of this section, students can: Deliver a working or simulated technology project supported by evidence, revision, and a technical explanation.

16. Control Systems and CNC

What students learn Why it matters
Model or build systems that use sensing, decisions, feedback, and safe states. Control systems show how sensing, decisions, and feedback produce safe, useful machine behaviour.

Subsections: Open and closed loop control; hysteresis; proportional control; PID; interlocks; motion and CNC

Coming out of this section, students can: Model or build a feedback-controlled system with safe states, interlocks, and measured response.
