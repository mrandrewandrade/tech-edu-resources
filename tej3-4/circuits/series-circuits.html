Series Circuits

Student notes, equations, slides, and questions for one-path direct-current circuits.

You will identify a series circuit and calculate total resistance, current, voltage drops, and power. This lets you predict electrical values before building and check whether measurements make sense.

Used in college or university: Direct-current circuit analysis and circuit laboratories. [1, Ch. 4]

Where you see it: In a car, a lamp circuit can form one path through the battery, fuse, switch, and lamp. In a robot, a status light and current-limiting resistor form the same kind of path.

Series and parallel

A series circuit has one path, so the current is the same everywhere. A parallel circuit has branches that share the same two nodes, so each branch has the same voltage.

Opening any component stops current everywhere.

General rules

\[ I_k = I_T \tag{1}\]

\[ R_T = \sum_{k=1}^{n} R_k \tag{2}\]

\[ V_T = \sum_{k=1}^{n} V_k \tag{3}\]

\[ P_T = \sum_{k=1}^{n} P_k = V_T I_T \tag{4}\]

\[ I_T = \frac{V_T}{R_T} \tag{5}\]

\[ V_k = I_T R_k \tag{6}\]

Here, \(k\) means any component in the path. Keep full calculator precision until the final answer.

\[ \sum_{k=1}^{n} V_k \overset{?}{=} V_T \tag{7}\]

Use Equation Equation 7 to check the voltage drops. Also check power ratings.

Calculate then simulate

Calculate the circuit first. Build the same series and parallel circuits in Tinkercad, measure current and voltage, then compare the calculated and simulated values.

Worked solutions

Questions 2 and 4 are provided as student models. Complete the other questions independently using the same GUESS structure.

NoteQuestion 2: Complete series analysis

Given: \(V_T=15.0\,\mathrm{V}\), \(R_1=1.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\), \(R_2=2.2\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\), \(R_3=3.3\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\).

\[ R_T=R_1+R_2+R_3=6.5\,\mathrm{k\Omega} \tag{8}\]

\[ I_T=\frac{V_T}{R_T}=\frac{15.0\,\mathrm{V}}{6.5\,\mathrm{k\Omega}}=2.307692\ldots\,\mathrm{mA} \tag{9}\]

Using \(V_k=I_TR_k\) gives \(V_1=2.307692\ldots\,\mathrm{V}\), \(V_2=5.076923\ldots\,\mathrm{V}\), and \(V_3=7.615385\ldots\,\mathrm{V}\).

Final answers: \(R_T=6.5\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\), \(I_T=2.3\,\mathrm{mA}\), \(V_1=2.3\,\mathrm{V}\), \(V_2=5.1\,\mathrm{V}\), \(V_3=7.6\,\mathrm{V}\), and \(P_T=35\,\mathrm{mW}\).

Check: Use the unrounded values: \(V_1+V_2+V_3=15.0\,\mathrm{V}\).

NoteQuestion 4: Choose a dropping resistor

Given: a \(6.0\,\mathrm{V}\), \(80\,\mathrm{mA}\) lamp on a \(9.0\,\mathrm{V}\) source.

\[ V_R=V_T-V_L=9.0\,\mathrm{V}-6.0\,\mathrm{V}=3.0\,\mathrm{V} \tag{10}\]

\[ R=\frac{V_R}{I}=\frac{3.0\,\mathrm{V}}{80\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{A}}=37.5\,\Omega \tag{11}\]

\[ P_R=V_RI=(3.0\,\mathrm{V})(80\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{A})=0.240\,\mathrm{W} \tag{12}\]

Final answer: the ideal value is \(38\,\Omega\). Choose a common \(39\,\Omega\) resistor rated for at least \(0.5\,\mathrm{W}\).

Key note: A \(0.25\,\mathrm{W}\) resistor is too close to the calculated \(0.240\,\mathrm{W}\) load.
