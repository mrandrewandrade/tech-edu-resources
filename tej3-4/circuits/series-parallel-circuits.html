Series-Parallel Combination Circuits, Part A

Student notes, equations, slides, questions, and chapter support for reducing and solving mixed resistor networks.

Assignment 1.4

You will identify series and parallel groups, reduce a mixed circuit one group at a time, calculate the total values, and then work back to each resistor. This is useful when one protected power source feeds several loads in a robot, vehicle, computer, or sensor system.

Used in college or university: circuit laboratories, electronics, robotics, instrumentation, and preparation for Kirchhoff’s laws. The same circuit pattern lets cars and robots power several loads from one protected source.

Where you see it: A car uses a fuse and switch to feed parallel lamps or control modules. A robot uses one protected supply for sensors, indicators, and control boards.

NoteRead the schematic

Higher voltage is at the top and the 0 V or ground return is at the bottom. The battery’s long line is the positive terminal. Follow conventional current from the positive rail through the series section, through each branch, and back to ground.

Start with a real system

A robot may have a fuse, switch, and current-limiting resistor in series with several parallel loads. A vehicle uses the same pattern when one protected supply feeds lamps, sensors, or control modules. The series parts affect every load; each parallel branch still receives the same branch voltage.

For an engaging introduction, watch ElectroBOOM101-005, then use The Organic Chemistry Tutor’s series and parallel explanation or Khan Academy’s resistor-network review for the calculation steps.

Identify the connections

Components are in series when the same current must pass through them. Components are in parallel when both ends connect to the same two nodes. Trace nodes and current paths; do not decide from visual appearance alone.

Rules and equations

For a series group, current is common, voltage drops add, and resistance adds:

\[ I_T=I_1=I_2=\cdots \tag{1}\]

\[ V_T=\sum_{k=1}^{n}V_k \tag{2}\]

\[ R_{\mathrm{series}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}R_k \tag{3}\]

For a parallel group, voltage is common, branch currents add, and reciprocal resistance adds:

\[ V_T=V_1=V_2=\cdots \tag{4}\]

\[ I_T=\sum_{k=1}^{n}I_k \tag{5}\]

\[ \frac{1}{R_{\mathrm{parallel}}} =\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{R_k} \tag{6}\]

For exactly two parallel resistors:

\[ R_{\mathrm{parallel}}=\frac{R_aR_b}{R_a+R_b} \tag{7}\]

For any resistor, use Ohm’s law and power:

\[ V_k=I_kR_k \tag{8}\]

\[ P_k=V_kI_k=I_k^2R_k=\frac{V_k^2}{R_k} \tag{9}\]

How sigma notation works

\[ R_T=R_{eq}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}R_k=R_1+R_2+\cdots+R_n \tag{10}\]

\[ V_T=\sum_{k=1}^{n}V_k=V_1+V_2+\cdots+V_n \tag{11}\]

Symbol Meaning
\(R_T\) or \(R_{eq}\) total or equivalent resistance
\(R_k\) or \(V_k\) the value at position \(k\)
\(k\) the index variable; it changes through \(1,2,3,\ldots,n\)
\(n\) the fixed last index; the sum stops here

\(\Sigma\) means add each term while \(k\) runs from \(1\) to \(n\). The final expanded term is \(R_n\) or \(V_n\).

Reduce, solve, expand, and check

  1. Mark one true series or parallel group.
  2. Replace it with an equivalent resistance and redraw the whole circuit.
  3. Repeat until only total resistance remains.
  4. Solve the equivalent circuit with \(I_T=V_T/R_T\).
  5. Work backward. Use common current for series parts and common voltage for parallel branches.
  6. Check that branch currents add, voltage drops add around a path, and component powers add to total power.

Question 1 worked example

A \(12.0\,\mathrm{V}\) source supplies \(R_1=1.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\) in series with \(R_2=2.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\) and \(R_3=2.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\) in parallel. This is the complete model for the questions that follow.

Given

\[ V_T=12.0\,\mathrm{V},\qquad R_1=1.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega},\qquad R_2=R_3=2.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega} \tag{12}\]

Unknowns

\[ R_{23},\ R_T,\ I_T,\ V_1,\ V_{23},\ I_2,\ I_3,\ P_T \tag{13}\]

General equations

\[ R_{\mathrm{parallel}}=\frac{R_aR_b}{R_a+R_b},\qquad R_{\mathrm{series}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}R_k,\qquad I=\frac{V}{R} \tag{14}\]

\[ V=IR,\qquad P=VI \tag{15}\]

Circuit-specific equations

\[ R_{23}=\frac{R_2R_3}{R_2+R_3},\qquad R_T=R_1+R_{23},\qquad I_T=\frac{V_T}{R_T} \tag{16}\]

\[ V_1=I_TR_1,\qquad V_{23}=V_T-V_1,\qquad I_2=\frac{V_{23}}{R_2},\qquad I_3=\frac{V_{23}}{R_3} \tag{17}\]

Step 1: Equivalent resistance

\[ R_{23}=\frac{(2.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega})(2.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega})} {2.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}+2.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}} =1.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega} \tag{18}\]

\[ R_T=1.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}+1.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega} =2.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega} \tag{19}\]

Step 2: Total current and section voltages

\[ I_T=\frac{12.0\,\mathrm{V}}{2.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}} =6.0\,\mathrm{mA} \tag{20}\]

\[ V_1=(6.0\,\mathrm{mA})(1.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega})=6.0\,\mathrm{V} \tag{21}\]

\[ V_{23}=12.0\,\mathrm{V}-6.0\,\mathrm{V}=6.0\,\mathrm{V} \tag{22}\]

Step 3: Branch currents and power

\[ I_2=I_3=\frac{6.0\,\mathrm{V}}{2.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}} =3.0\,\mathrm{mA} \tag{23}\]

\[ P_T=V_TI_T=(12.0\,\mathrm{V})(6.0\,\mathrm{mA}) =72\,\mathrm{mW} \tag{24}\]

Calculator entries

(2 EXP 3 × 2 EXP 3) ÷ (2 EXP 3 + 2 EXP 3)
12 ÷ (2 EXP 3)
6 ÷ (2 EXP 3)

Step 4: Check

\[ I_2+I_3=3.0\,\mathrm{mA}+3.0\,\mathrm{mA}=6.0\,\mathrm{mA}=I_T \tag{25}\]

\[ V_1+V_{23}=6.0\,\mathrm{V}+6.0\,\mathrm{V}=12.0\,\mathrm{V}=V_T \tag{26}\]

Final answers

Quantity \(R_{23}\) \(R_T\) \(I_T\) \(V_1\) \(V_{23}\) \(I_2\) \(I_3\) \(P_T\)
Answer \(1.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\) \(2.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\) \(6.0\,\mathrm{mA}\) \(6.0\,\mathrm{V}\) \(6.0\,\mathrm{V}\) \(3.0\,\mathrm{mA}\) \(3.0\,\mathrm{mA}\) \(72\,\mathrm{mW}\)

Question 2: unequal parallel branches

An \(18.0\,\mathrm{V}\) source supplies \(R_1=3.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\) in series with \(R_2=6.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\) and \(R_3=3.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\) in parallel. Find \(R_{23}\), \(R_T\), \(I_T\), \(V_1\), \(V_{23}\), \(I_2\), \(I_3\), and \(P_T\). Check current and voltage.

Question 3: two series branches in parallel

A \(24.0\,\mathrm{V}\) source is connected across two branches. Branch A has \(R_1=1.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\) and \(R_2=3.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\) in series. Branch B has \(R_3=2.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\) and \(R_4=4.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\) in series. Find the branch resistances and currents, every resistor voltage, total current, equivalent resistance, and total power.

Question 4: find an unknown branch resistor

A \(20.0\,\mathrm{V}\) source supplies \(R_1=1.2\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\) in series with a parallel group. One branch is \(R_2=2.4\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\) and the other is an unknown \(R_3\). The source current is \(10.0\,\mathrm{mA}\). Find \(V_1\), \(V_{23}\), \(I_2\), \(I_3\), \(R_3\), and \(R_T\).

Question 5: current-limit design

A \(12.0\,\mathrm{V}\) source supplies \(R_S\), \(R_1=330\,\mathrm{\Omega}\), and a parallel load made from \(R_2=680\,\mathrm{\Omega}\) and \(R_3=1.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\). Find the minimum \(R_S\) that keeps source current at or below \(15.0\,\mathrm{mA}\). Select the next safe standard 5% value, then calculate current, load voltage, branch currents, resistor powers, and suitable power ratings.

Question 1 is the complete example. Questions 2 and 4 are additional models. Complete Questions 3 and 5 independently.

NoteQuestion 2: GUESS solution

Given: \(V_T=18.0\,\mathrm{V}\), \(R_1=3.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\), \(R_2=6.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\), \(R_3=3.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\).

Unknowns: \(R_{23}\), \(R_T\), \(I_T\), \(V_1\), \(V_{23}\), \(I_2\), \(I_3\), \(P_T\).

General equations: use Equations Equation 7, Equation 3, Equation 8, Equation 5, and Equation 9.

Substitute and solve

\[ R_{23}=\frac{(6.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega})(3.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega})} {6.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}+3.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}} =2.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega} \tag{27}\]

\[ R_T=3.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}+2.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega} =5.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega} \tag{28}\]

\[ I_T=\frac{18.0\,\mathrm{V}}{5.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}} =3.60\,\mathrm{mA} \tag{29}\]

\[ V_1=(3.60\,\mathrm{mA})(3.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}) =10.8\,\mathrm{V} \tag{30}\]

\[ V_{23}=18.0\,\mathrm{V}-10.8\,\mathrm{V} =7.2\,\mathrm{V} \tag{31}\]

\[ I_2=\frac{7.2\,\mathrm{V}}{6.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}} =1.20\,\mathrm{mA},\qquad I_3=\frac{7.2\,\mathrm{V}}{3.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}} =2.40\,\mathrm{mA} \tag{32}\]

\[ P_T=(18.0\,\mathrm{V})(3.60\,\mathrm{mA}) =64.8\,\mathrm{mW} \tag{33}\]

Statement: \(R_{23}=2.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\), \(R_T=5.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\), \(I_T=3.60\,\mathrm{mA}\), \(V_1=10.8\,\mathrm{V}\), \(V_{23}=7.2\,\mathrm{V}\), \(I_2=1.20\,\mathrm{mA}\), \(I_3=2.40\,\mathrm{mA}\), and \(P_T=64.8\,\mathrm{mW}\).

Check: \(I_2+I_3=I_T\) and \(V_1+V_{23}=V_T\).

NoteQuestion 4: GUESS solution

Given: \(V_T=20.0\,\mathrm{V}\), \(R_1=1.2\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\), \(R_2=2.4\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\), \(I_T=10.0\,\mathrm{mA}\).

Unknowns: \(V_1\), \(V_{23}\), \(I_2\), \(I_3\), \(R_3\), \(R_T\).

General equations

\[ V_1=I_TR_1,\qquad V_{23}=V_T-V_1,\qquad I_2=\frac{V_{23}}{R_2} \tag{34}\]

\[ I_3=I_T-I_2,\qquad R_3=\frac{V_{23}}{I_3},\qquad R_T=\frac{V_T}{I_T} \tag{35}\]

Substitute and solve

\[ V_1=(10.0\,\mathrm{mA})(1.2\,\mathrm{k\Omega}) =12.0\,\mathrm{V} \tag{36}\]

\[ V_{23}=20.0\,\mathrm{V}-12.0\,\mathrm{V} =8.0\,\mathrm{V} \tag{37}\]

\[ I_2=\frac{8.0\,\mathrm{V}}{2.4\,\mathrm{k\Omega}} =3.333333\ldots\,\mathrm{mA} \tag{38}\]

\[ I_3=10.0\,\mathrm{mA}-3.333333\ldots\,\mathrm{mA} =6.666667\ldots\,\mathrm{mA} \tag{39}\]

\[ R_3=\frac{8.0\,\mathrm{V}}{6.666667\,\mathrm{mA}} =1.20\,\mathrm{k\Omega} \tag{40}\]

\[ R_T=\frac{20.0\,\mathrm{V}}{10.0\,\mathrm{mA}} =2.00\,\mathrm{k\Omega} \tag{41}\]

Statement: \(V_1=12.0\,\mathrm{V}\), \(V_{23}=8.0\,\mathrm{V}\), \(I_2=3.33\,\mathrm{mA}\), \(I_3=6.67\,\mathrm{mA}\), \(R_3=1.20\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\), and \(R_T=2.00\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\).

Check: \(I_2+I_3=10.0\,\mathrm{mA}=I_T\).

Lab connection

Use Experiment 6-1 to verify equivalent resistance before applying power. Continue with Experiment 6-2 or Experiment 6-3 to compare calculated and measured values.

Equipment: breadboard, digital multimeter, low-voltage direct-current supply, and leads.

Consumables: the resistor values specified by the selected experiment.

Evidence: circuit photo, measured-value table, calculated-value table, percent differences, and one brief explanation of the largest difference.

Safety: switch off the supply before moving components or measuring resistance. Measure current with the meter placed in series, never directly across a source.

Teachers can email Andrew for the complete answer key.

Practical pathway: reduce, build, expand, check

Core learning: keep the existing Assignment 1.4 and worked models. After the calculation, build the same topology using the Tinkercad tutorial.

Guided practice: label the series resistor and parallel group before wiring. Record total current, the parallel-group voltage, and both branch currents.
