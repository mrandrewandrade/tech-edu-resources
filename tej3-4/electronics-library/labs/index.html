Grob experiment suggestions

Use these as starting points rather than automatic assignments. Each lesson package should select the experiment that actually verifies its learning goal, adapt it to current equipment and protected low-voltage practice, or state that no experiment is needed.

Engineering notation and calculator skills Introduction Experiment: Electronics Math Scientific calculator and TEJ reference None Calculation prompts; no circuit parts
Electrical quantities and safe setup Experiment 1-1: Lab Safety, Equipment, and Components Protected low-voltage source, digital multimeter, breadboard, resistor and component samples Tinkercad optional Label components and prepare one known-safe demonstration circuit
Resistance and resistor identification Experiments 2-1 and 2-2 Digital multimeter, resistor assortment, breadboard, protected source Tinkercad optional Resistors with several values and tolerances; spare parts for damaged leads
Ohm’s law and power Experiments 3-1 and 3-2 Current-limited supply, digital multimeter, breadboard, rated resistors Tinkercad first; Multisim optional Choose values that remain below resistor power ratings
Series circuits Experiment 4-1 core; Experiment 4-2 extension Current-limited 10.0 V supply, fused digital multimeter, breadboard, test leads, jumpers, 1.0 kΩ, 2.2 kΩ, and 3.3 kΩ resistors None required; Tinkercad optional Calculate and measure current at four points, voltage drops, Kirchhoff’s voltage-law check, percent difference, and one circuit photo; do not create a deliberate hard short
Parallel circuits Experiments 5-1 to 5-4 Fused or current-limited supply, digital multimeter, breadboard, branch resistors Tinkercad or Multisim Prepare an open-branch fault and verify the total-current meter range
Series-parallel circuits Experiments 6-1 to 6-4 Supply, digital multimeter, breadboard, resistor network Multisim recommended before building Prepare one canonical network with labelled reduction stages

Required experiment record

For every experiment used in a lesson package, record the experiment number, purpose, mode, equipment, software, reusable parts, consumables, teacher preparation, student evidence, safety boundary, and any adaptation from the published procedure.

The common introductory kit is a current-limited low-voltage source, fused digital multimeter, breadboard, insulated leads, resistor assortment, switches, protected loads, calculator, and TEJ electronics reference. Add specialized parts only when the experiment requires them.
